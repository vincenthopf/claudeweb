The Architecture of Manipulation

These insights enable "dark patterns" — interfaces designed to exploit human psychology:

Example 1: The Urgency Illusion

⚠️ Only 2 spots left! 23:45

How it works: Artificial scarcity creates pressure. The timer is fake—it resets for every visitor. Your hesitation data tells us exactly how long to make the timer to maximize conversion.

Example 2: The Infinite Scroll Trap

How it works: Your scroll velocity tells us when you're in a "flow state"—and that's when we remove stopping points. Infinite scroll exploits your momentum, keeping you engaged past your intended time.

Example 3: Confirmshaming

How it works: Emotional manipulation makes declining feel shameful. Your mouse movement data shows us which phrases trigger the strongest emotional response.

The Question

If you can see it happening, does that make it acceptable?

This page collected real data about your behavior. It tracked you, analyzed you, profiled you. But it showed you what it was doing.

Most websites don't.

They collect the same data—often more—but hide it behind "Accept Cookies" buttons and 40-page privacy policies designed to be incomprehensible.

Transparency doesn't equal consent. Visibility doesn't equal ethics.

The Cost

Data points collected: 0
Third-party trackers (typical site): 47
Your attention traded for: $0.00083

The average person's data is worth $0.00083 per page view to advertisers. But it's worth far more to you—it's your privacy, your autonomy, your psychological freedom.

What Can Be Done?

Individual Actions

  • Use privacy-focused browsers (Firefox, Brave)
  • Install tracker blockers (uBlock Origin, Privacy Badger)
  • Read privacy policies (or use tools like ToS;DR)
  • Question every "free" service (you're the product)

Collective Actions

  • Support strong privacy legislation (GDPR, CCPA)
  • Choose privacy-respecting alternatives
  • Demand transparency from platforms
  • Build and support ethical technology

For Designers & Developers

  • Practice ethical design—refuse dark patterns
  • Collect only necessary data
  • Make privacy policies readable
  • Build transparency into interfaces

A Note on This Project

This website tracked you, but kept all data local—nothing was sent to any server. No third parties have access to your behavioral data. When you close this tab, it disappears.

This is how it should be.

The web could be a space for creativity, connection, and genuine human interaction. Instead, it's become an extraction machine, optimized for capturing attention and harvesting data.

But it doesn't have to be this way.

Every website is a choice. Every interface is a political statement. Every line of code either respects or exploits its users.
